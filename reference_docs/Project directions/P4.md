## Overview

In this milestone, you will make the finishing touches to your personal project. You’ll check the behavior and access control of your app, polish the interface, deploy your app so that it can be accessed by others, and summarize your design notes.

## Tasks

**Review your reactions and access control.** Check that your reactions handle authentication and access control appropriately, and make any other changes you think improve the design. We recommend that you do this incrementally, checking each change as you go. As usual, record your design ideas and the rationale for your reactions as you work, in your design notebook.

**Polish your app.** Play with your app, adding some plausible data. Make whatever changes seem desirable to you, in visual aspects or behavior. Feel free to modify the reactions and even the concepts themselves. Record any significant changes and their rationale as you work.

**Make a final video.** Make a short video (up to 3 minutes in length) showing your app in use and highlighting its key features, with an audio narration throughout. You should show at least some intelligible user journey (for example, the one you used before) and can also point to features without demonstrating them fully.

**Complete your design document.** Turn your design notes, which record your ideas, changes, and observations, into a coherent document of one to two pages. Summarize how your final design differs from your initial concept design in P1 and your visual design in P3. Be succinct and to the point, and use bullets and subheadings to make the document easy to navigate. Feel free to use diagrams and screenshots to illustrate your points if helpful.

To refer to particular versions of concept specifications and prompts, use links to particular Git commits.

**Prepare for user testing.** Ensure that your app is usable and understandable not just to you, but to others. In P5, you will conduct user tests.

**Update your README.** Make sure your README provides a brief overview of your project (one or two sentences suffice), clearly organizes and links to relevant project materials (such as design documents, specifications, and demos), and includes instructions for building, running, and testing your app locally. Your README should be concise and easy to navigate. A well-written README is an essential part of making software accessible and reproducible, and documenting the setup process can also help you identify missing configuration steps.

## Deliverables

- Your finalized front-end and back-end code in your project repository, including current specifications, reactions, tests, and design notes.

- The URL of your deployed app.

- Your design document.

- Your video.

- The design document and the link to your demo video in one or more Markdown files, placed in the project repository and clearly linked from the README.

For P4, test the deployed application, including persistence, access control, and error handling. Your design summary should explain the significant decisions behind the final app.

## Rubrics

P4 assesses the following skills:

- [**Implementing a concept-based application**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/implementing-concepts/): front-end and back-end code, current concept specifications and reactions, integration, and design decisions documented throughout development.

- [**Testing and validating software**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/testing-validating/): concept tests, integration tests, validation of the deployed app, and the narrated demo video.

- [**Interaction design**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/interaction-design/): user journeys and interactions within the app, including navigation, feedback, error handling, and ease of use.

- [**Visual design**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/visual-design/): the app’s interface, including layout, typography, consistency, visual hierarchy, and readability.

- [**Technical writing**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/technical-writing/): the design document and design notes, including explanations of significant decisions and changes to earlier milestones.

- [**Professionalism**](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/professionalism/): the README, repository organization, Git history, and reproducibility of the project.
