# Rubric for Reflective Practice

Source: [Rubric for Reflective Practice](https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/reflective-practice/)  
Downloaded: October 5, 2026

This rubric applies to all reflection activities, including personal goals, lessons from personal projects, collaboration notes, etc. Note that reflecting well does not mean that your conclusions have to all be positive! On the contrary, the best reflections are often those that point to things that went wrong and try to diagnose them so you can be more successful in the future.

A good reflection has these characteristics:

- **Focus**. The reflection is focused and directed with one or more purposes in mind. These might include: laying out personal goals for an activity or the class as a whole; extracting lessons from a project to inform the next project you do; gaining insight into opportunities for personal improvement; recording current thoughts and feelings to be read later to measure progress; and so on.
- **Grounding and specificity**. The reflection supports its observations with relevant details from personal experience, an activity, a goal, a decision, or other appropriate evidence.
- **Depth**. The analysis should draw non-trivial insights, and not just be a superficial accounting and tallying of successes and failures.
- **Self awareness**. The reflection should demonstrate---and contribute to---an awareness of your own abilities, habits, limitations, needs and preferences, and so on.
- **Integration**. The reflection should not be a collection of unrelated observations, but should create a coherent whole. One way to test this is to ask yourself if you could repeat your reflection orally, without referring to the text.
- **Forward thinking**. The reflection should have clear lessons for the future, and ideally calls to action that guide future behavior.

Common pitfalls:

- **Impersonal**. The reflection contains no real insight about the person who did the work or how they did and the challenges they encountered, but could have been inferred by looking at the work product alone. This is why asking an LLM to read your work and write a reflection will usually produce a bad outcome.
- **Disconnected**. The reflection contains personal insights, but they are not grounded in the activity. A good reflection on an activity connects the feelings of the writer to the details of the activity.
- **Rambling**. The reflection isn't crisp and organized, but rambles around between topics, and feels repetitive and unfocused.
- **Shallow**. The reflection is cast in terms of low level details, and doesn't provide any deeper insights into the learning process of the writer.

Competency levels:

- **Deficient**. The reflection is vague and doesn't include any concrete observations or lessons.
- **Emergent**. The reflection includes observations about the activity and connects them to the writer, but doesn't draw useful lessons.
- **Competent**. The reflection offers a coherent and personal account of an activity and draws useful lessons from it.
- **Expert**. The reflection provides real insight about the writer that will be helpful in guiding future activities, and adds real value to the experience of the activity itself.
