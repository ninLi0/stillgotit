# P5 session kit

What the facilitator used for the two sessions described on the [P5 page](P5.md). The task list is on that page and is not repeated here.

## Before the session

- The deployed app at the commit the P5 page pins, with the laptop signed in to the owner's account. The message box's model pill reads Gemini 4 Argon at Medium, and "Interactive examples" is off, so participants see plain answers unless they turn it on.
- The account holds two courses filled with real materials and real tutor activity, 6.3930 and Reggae and World Music, made with the [seeding script](../scripts/seed-in-page.md): confirmed skills in several topics, lessons with follow-ups, assessed practice, a written adjustment, a budget, a dated exam with an active plan and a decided trade-off, a tree of another kind, and an archived conversation. Older courses sit archived in the switcher.
- The task course, Intro to Investing, created empty, and the reading short-sales-reading.pdf on the Desktop.
- Screen recording with the microphone on, a timer the participant cannot see, and the note sheet below open beside the task list.

## Script

### Consent

"Thank you for doing this. I am testing a study app I built for a class, and I want to see how it works for someone who has never seen it. I would like to record the screen and your voice while you use it, so I can go back over what happened. The recording stays with me and is not shared. You can stop at any time and you do not need a reason. Is that all right?"

Then: "You will be using my account, so nothing about you is stored in the app. The app sends the course material and the messages you type to Google's Gemini service to produce its answers, so please do not type anything private."

### Brief

"I am going to give you seven short tasks, one at a time. I am testing the app, not you: if something is confusing, that is a problem with the app, and it is exactly what I need to find. Please think out loud the whole time: say what you are trying to do, what you expect to happen, and read out what you are looking at. I will mostly stay quiet."

Warm-up: "Before the first task, just look at this page and tell me what you think it is for."

### Tasks

Read each instruction from the P5 page's table, as written. Rules fixed in advance:

- Silence longer than about fifteen seconds: "What are you thinking now?"
- Help only after two minutes without progress, or a second request, and then the smallest hint, never an explanation of the interface. Every hint is written down with the task and the time.
- A task is over when the participant says they are done. A task they believe done but is not is noted, not corrected.

What to watch for:

1. Where they look first to add a material, and how they tell the file is ready.
2. Whether "Find skills" is found, and what they think confirming a skill will do.
3. What they say the percentage means, and whether they open the (i) notes.
4. Whether citations are noticed, followed and trusted.
5. Whether the help declaration is understood, and whether the verdict's reason is read.
6. The order in which budget, exam and schedule are found, and whether the trade-off reads as a choice. It appears only with a 15-minute budget and a quiz tomorrow.
7. Whether the Memory view is found, what they say the curve means, and how long the tree picker takes to find.

### Debrief

- "What is this app for, in your own words?"
- "What worked well? What would you change first?"
- For each hesitation in the notes: "You paused here. What were you trying to do? What did you expect? What did the app do instead? How did you work it out?"
- "When the tutor cited the reading, did you trust it? Did you check?"
- "What did the percentage on a skill mean to you? What did the curve mean?"
- "Was there anything you did not understand the words for?"
- "Would you use this for a class you are taking now? Why or why not?"
