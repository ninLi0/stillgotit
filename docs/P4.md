## What changed since P3, and why

P3 ended with a beta that had every feature from the P1 pitch except the source-search fallback. P4 asked for a review of reactions and access control, for polish, and for a final design. The work since P3 therefore has three parts: a review of who can reach what, a revamp of the concepts that answers the TA feedback on P1 and P2, and a pass of small improvements to the interface.

### Reviewing reactions and access control

- **The audit found no path to another learner's data.** On October 25, every endpoint in the compositions and every route in the host was traced by reading the code. For each one, the audit recorded how a request without a session is refused, which ownership view guards the endpoint, and what happens when a request names another learner's record. No endpoint lets a signed-in learner read or change another learner's course, skill, session, case, material, or plan. Requests without a session are refused everywhere except the public steps of Commons sign-in, the health check, and the static files.
- **Every learning and planning command now answers a refusal** (`144c53b`). The audit found fifteen commands with no refusal of their own. Over HTTP and through the exported gateway, admission refused them first, with a sentence, so learners never saw the gap. A caller that skipped admission, however, such as the host's own code, a test, or a route added later without an admission entry, got no answer at all: nothing ran, and the request waited 90 seconds for its time limit. Each of these commands now answers with an existing public code when its first guard fails. Lesson and Task answer `SKILL_UNAVAILABLE`. Respond, Question, Reply, Hint, Assess, and RetryAssessment answer `SESSION_UNAVAILABLE`. Regrade answers `REVISION_CASE_UNAVAILABLE`, and Suggest answers `MATERIAL_UNAVAILABLE` or `COURSE_UNAVAILABLE`. In a course the learner owns, Begin, Finish, Stop, and Miss answer the planning concept's code for an unknown or foreign activity, and Propose answers when the course has no budget or an activity is in progress. New tests send another learner's real identifiers, both over HTTP and directly to the engine.
- **Correct now decides with a positive condition** (`144c53b`). `/learning/correct` chose its practice branch with the condition "the case is not an imported case". That condition also held when the case was not the learner's at all. The concept then refused the change, so nothing was saved, but the composition had not made the decision. The branch now requires the learner's own practice case, and a separate branch refuses a case that is not the learner's.
- **Replans wait for an activity in any course** (`6132f94`). Seven reactions ask for a new plan: after new evidence, a confidence report, a budget change, a finished, stopped, or missed activity, and an estimate adjustment. While an activity was in progress in one course, the planning concept refused a new plan for every course, so a budget change in a second course was saved but answered with an error. These reactions now skip while an activity is in progress in any course, as the exam reactions already did, and the plan is updated when the activity ends. The estimate adjustment says so in its answer.

### The concept revamp

Each item was built on its own branch and merged into `main` on October 25. The [design document](design-document.md) explains the reasons in more detail.

- **MaterialKeeping takes the material library out of GroundedTutoring** (merge `d66d918`). GroundedTutoring used to keep the learner's materials as well as the lessons and practice sessions that use them. These are two concerns, and the P1 TA had pointed out that the concepts mixed concerns. MaterialKeeping, used in the app as `Library`, now keeps each learner's materials, their readable content, their retained files, and their import retries. GroundedTutoring checks only what it owns: a session names its materials, and its citations name those materials. The ownership and same-course checks moved into the compositions. On its first start after the deploy, the server renames the deployed collection `groundedtutoring.materials` to `library.materials`, so no material is lost.
- **SkillMapping keeps one proposal key per skill** (merge `6a483f5`). A retried draft save must not create a second draft. SkillMapping used to keep a separate receipt for every proposal, with ten fields and a two-step protocol. Each skill now carries an optional `proposalKey`, and a unique index on the unit and key pair makes a repeated save return the skill it already created. Two refusal codes went away with the receipts. The requirement versions stay, because the version history is what makes revising a skill safe for work already done. The rules for writing records in a safe order moved from the specification to the [runtime responsibilities](../design/runtime.md#safe-writes-without-transactions).
- **StudyPlanning became an instance of Budgeting** (merge `2a7a851`). The planning concept stored seven totals, such as the minutes an exam needs and whether its cuts need confirmation, that could be computed from its other records. Its queries now compute them. The concept was also renamed Budgeting, with general words: an owner, deadlines, and the required and available minutes. Nothing in its rules needed the words study or exam. The app still calls its instance StudyPlanning, so the database collections did not change, and the routes, reactions, and screens still say exam, because in this app every deadline is an exam.
- **Every reaction is written as when, where, then** (`fbc43b2`, merged in `f7b34be`, and `508b933`). The P1 TA said that the reactions hid their complexity in prose. The progress reactions in [Progress.md](../design/compositions/Progress.md), the plan activation in [Planning.md](../design/compositions/Planning.md), and the repair reaction in [Courses.md](../design/compositions/Courses.md) now state the action that triggers each one, the conditions it reads, and the actions it takes.
- **A page names the host's actors** (`fbc43b2`, merged in `f7b34be`). The P2 TA said that the external functionality was not in a legible pattern. [design/host.md](../design/host.md) lists each part of the server outside the concepts, the file that implements it, what it owns, what it calls or serves, and the page that describes it.
- **Admission and the endpoints read the same conditions** (`7f6e793`, merged with the refusal work in `3479973`). The P2 TA asked why the learning endpoints were duplicated instead of having a refusal branch. Each endpoint repeated the conditions that its admission former also stated. These conditions are now stated once, as two shared views and four condition helpers that both sides use, and [LearningAccess.md](../design/compositions/LearningAccess.md) answers the question: admission adds a sentence and stops a command before any work starts, and the endpoint's own refusal answers every other caller. The page also lists the differences between the two that remain on purpose.

### Polish

Each improvement was built on its own branch and merged into `main` on October 25 and 26.

- **Home shows what is in progress** (`1a8ae10`, merged in `c9fb257`). A Continue card lists the learner's open lessons and practice tasks across their courses, newest first, and each row opens that session.
- **A course can be archived and restored** (`38f4150`, merged in `2b423b5`). Archiving asks for confirmation, keeps the course's materials, skills, progress, and plan, and moves it to an "Archived courses" fold in the course menu, from which Restore brings it back. A course with an activity in progress refuses to archive until the activity ends.
- **A saved draft skill can be discarded** (`81bb934`, merged in `2b423b5`). Discard used to work only for suggestions that were not yet saved.
- **A lesson can be finished** (`8bbe681`, merged in `34fae07`). Lessons used to stay open forever, so Continue listed every one. Finishing closes the lesson, keeps its conversation readable, and drops it from Continue. A finished lesson cannot be reopened; the learner starts a new conversation instead.
- **Transcripts import as readable paragraphs** (`65a0525`, merged in `db5bf42`; `0a17a94`, `0c6acaf`, and `e7aaf34`, merged in `c709a53`; `2475334`, merged in `5428bd0`). A VTT or SRT caption file used to appear in Readable text as one cue per line with its timestamps. The cues are now joined into paragraphs, a line that repeats the end of the previous cue is left out, a name that starts most cues becomes a speaker tag, and a paragraph breaks after about a minute of speech. The times themselves are not kept.
- **The tutor's conversation shows what each reply cost** (`a21ad4e` and `dd84fb6`, merged in `7c81cb9`; `3b29626` and `80fa5d7`, merged in `ea79239`; `fb1b50a`). Under each reply, a line gives the time to answer, the number of sources cited, and the model and thinking level, with Copy and Listen beside it, and the sources it cited appear as chips. An assessment shows the mastery change, such as "50% → 95%". The thread itself was laid out like a modern assistant's: the opening in a card, a divider, then alternating messages.
- **Mastery is drawn as well as counted** (`aa70ffe`, `c6eaf17`, and `2128215`, merged in `ae5562f`). The evidence panel draws the estimate over time, the Forest view summarizes a course by topic, with the average, the counts of mastered, growing, new, and due skills, and the evidence timeline shows one dated row per change.
- **Every public refusal code has a sentence** (`60bed47`, merged in `c9fb257`). The last four, for a session or skill that changed or is unavailable and for a material that is unavailable, used to reach the browser as a general message.
- **Every small text passes 4.5:1 contrast** (`d97367b`, merged in `c9fb257`; `b0c03e5`). The [visual design study](../design/visual-design.md#contrast) lists the pairs that were changed.

## How the work was checked

- **Checks.** After each merge, the generated files were rebuilt, and `bun run check` passed. It checks 12 concept instances against their specifications and reports 74 advisories, the same two kinds as in P3.
- **Tests.** The full test suite passed after each merge that changed code. After the last merge (`2a646ef`), it passed 976 tests across 81 files, with 9,466 assertions and no failures, in 159 seconds, with nothing else running. Earlier runs while other suites ran in parallel showed that the test of an upload that runs out of time depended on a 300-millisecond budget that a busy machine cannot meet, so its budget now matches its neighbour's 1,500 milliseconds; the test still times out inside the held file write it is about.
- **The interface with canned answers.** On the merged code, the browser fixture with canned tutor answers ran the whole journey: adding a material, proposing and confirming skills, a lesson, a budget, an exam, confirming a trade-off, practice, and an assessment. After the polish merges, the same fixture showed the transcript paragraphs, the draft discard, the reply statistics, the Continue card, the course progress summary, the mastery change on an assessment, the estimate chart and dated timeline, and archiving and restoring a course.
- **The interface with the real tutor.** The same fixture, run with the real Gemini key, added a material, suggested three skills, confirmed one, gave a lesson answer that cited the material, created a practice task, and saved an assessment.

## Testing the deployed app

These checks run on https://stillgotit.mit-sdg.dev after the deploy. They need two Commons accounts, called A and B here, in two separate browser profiles.

**Persistence.**

1. Before the deploy, sign in as A and note each course and its materials, skills, estimates, exams, and plan.
2. After the deploy, sign in as A again. Check that every course and material from step 1 is still listed and that each material opens. This also checks the one-time move of the materials into `library.materials`.
3. Create a course, add a short PDF, find skills, confirm one, set its starting confidence, set a daily budget of 20 minutes, and add an exam one week away that covers the skill. In Settings, choose a tutor model and the task list as the Home view.
4. Sign out from Settings, sign in again, and reload each screen: Home, Plan, Materials, and the skill's lesson. Check that everything from step 3 is still there.

**Access control.**

1. As A, open the browser's developer tools and copy, from the responses in the Network panel, the identifiers of A's course, a skill, a material, a lesson or practice session, and an assessed case.
2. As B, open the app, and in the developer tools console define a helper that sends a request from the app's own page:

   ```js
   const ask = (path, body) => fetch(`/api${path}`, { method: "POST", headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" }, body: JSON.stringify(body) }).then(async (r) => [r.status, await r.text()]);
   ```

3. Send each of these with A's identifiers, and check that each is refused with an error code and no data:
   - the course: `await ask("/courses/read", { course: "…" })` and `await ask("/planning/configure", { course: "…", dailyMinutes: 5, focusNote: "" })`;
   - the skill: `await ask("/learning/read", { skill: "…" })` and `await ask("/courses/skills/details", { skill: "…", title: "Changed by B", unit: "…" })`;
   - the material: `await ask("/courses/materials/read", { material: "…" })`;
   - the session: `await ask("/learning/transcript", { studySession: "…", turnIndex: 0, text: "Changed by B" })`;
   - the case: `await ask("/learning/correct", { case: "…", basedOn: "…", result: "INDEPENDENT", gateMet: true, reason: "Changed by B" })`.
4. Sign out as B and send one of the requests again. It should be refused because no one is signed in.
5. As A, reload and check that the course, skill, material, session, and case are unchanged.

**Error handling.**

1. Try three actions that the app refuses, and check that each shows a sentence that says what to do: renaming a skill to a blank name in Manage skills, starting practice on a skill whose prerequisite is not ready, and uploading a ZIP file.
2. Lose a reply and retry it. In Materials, ask the tutor to find skills, open the developer tools, set the network to a slow speed, and press the button that saves all suggestions as drafts, such as "Save all 3 as drafts". Reload the page before the save finishes. If the page then shows "Finish saving skills", press "Retry saving drafts". Check that each suggestion appears once as a draft.
3. Do the same with an estimate adjustment on a skill page: reload while it says "Saving your adjustment", press "Send adjustment again" if the panel offers it, and check that the evidence timeline shows the adjustment once.

## Preparing for user testing

P5 asks for user tests with people who have not seen the app. A tester needs a 6.1040 Commons account, one course they are taking, one material from that course, such as lecture notes or a problem set as a PDF, and about 20 minutes. The tester should not upload anything private, because course materials and answers are sent to Google's Gemini service. The setup screen says so as well.

Each session asks the tester to complete three journeys without help:

1. **Set up a course.** Create a course, add the material, check the text the app could read, ask the tutor to find skills, and confirm two of them.
2. **Learn and check a skill.** Set a starting confidence, open a lesson and ask for a worked example, then ask the tutor to check understanding, answer the practice, and send it for assessment. Then find out why the estimate changed.
3. **Plan the week.** Set a daily budget, add an exam with its date and the skills it covers, and decide what to do about any cuts the plan proposes.

## Known limits

**Importing and materials**

- When an upload is refused while it is being read, because it has too many pages, is damaged, or has nothing readable, its files stay in storage, and there is no way to remove them.
- When one upload contains identical files, their text appears more than once in the material.
- GIF, BMP, TIFF, and AVIF images are refused. Excel dates appear as serial numbers.
- A learner cannot delete a material once it is imported, and photos of the learner's own work are listed with the course materials.
- The 80-second limit for one tutor call and the memory bound for the largest upload are tested with short stand-in values, not at their real sizes.

**Graded work**

- One extraction reads at most 50 questions, so a longer paper must be split across uploads.
- Work that carries only a total score yields no rows, because a total cannot update individual skills.
- A PDF or photo larger than 15 MB cannot be read for marks.
- Uploading the same paper twice lets its questions be imported, and counted, twice.
- Whether a material is graded work is decided by its title, which starts with "Graded work: ".

**Skills, plans, and messages**

- Asking for skill suggestions again replaces suggestions the learner edited, without a warning, and an edit to a saved draft is saved only when the learner confirms the skill.
- Marking a unit as emphasized has no effect on the plan, and a course cannot be renamed.
- Each course has its own daily budget, and nothing limits the total across courses. After a learner declines a trade-off, the same cuts can come back after they finish or stop an activity in another course.
- A learner who has no material cannot choose a suggested reference.
- A finished lesson cannot be reopened, and its tab does not show that it is finished until it is opened.

**Tutor choice**

- An answer loses its model label after a reload, because the label is not stored.
- A reply's time to answer is measured by the page when the answer arrives. After a reload it is read from the saved times instead, and a reply whose question had to be retried shows none, because the marks of retried questions are kept in that browser only.
- Retrying a regrade after the provider refuses the request adds the learner's clarification a second time.
- Another browser's sidebar shows the old choice until the learner uses the tutor row there or reloads the page.
- Only Pro at High is marked as possibly too slow, because the timings of each model and level have not been measured.

**The design and the code**

- A few commands still wait for the time limit when a caller skips admission and the first guard passes but a later check fails: Begin with prerequisites that are not ready, Question on a practice session, Reply when there is no question, Regrade with an outdated judgment, a saved material that is no longer among the sources, and Retry of a case that has been judged. Admission refuses every one of these over HTTP and through the gateway.
- After a draft is discarded, replaying its original save creates it again. This needs a lost reply and a second tab at the same time.
- The startup step that renames the materials collection runs once on the next deploy, and it can be removed afterwards.
- The graded-work tests print engine warnings that a refusal answered a request after it had stopped waiting. The saved data is unchanged, but the path that answers twice has not been traced.
