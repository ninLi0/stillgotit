## What changed since P4

The app the participants used is not the one submitted for P4. The work finished after the P4 freeze and the work done for the sessions are listed with their commits on [their own page](changes-since-P4.md). In short: a lesson is saved before the tutor answers and can be continued, archived and found again later; a material can be deleted; a learner's own work is kept apart from the course's files; a graded paper counts once however often it is uploaded; the plan names the review that blocks it; plain reads no longer wait for the tutor; the interface says each fact once; the sign-in page fits a phone; an exam can cover a whole course in one click; and a seeding script fills a course with realistic activity.

## Preparing the deployed app

The participants cannot have their own accounts, because sign-in goes through the class Commons and they are not in the class. Each session therefore runs on the owner's laptop, signed in to the owner's account, with the owner's courses as the realistic surroundings and one fresh course for the tasks.

**Realistic surroundings.** Before the first session the account held two courses filled with the seeding script and the real tutor: 6.3930 (AI and Decision Making in Medicine), with its problem sets, lecture slides, project guidelines and a lecture transcript, and a music class with three published readings. Each has a dozen or so confirmed skills in three or four topics, lessons with follow-up questions, assessed practice attempts, a written adjustment, a daily budget, a dated exam with an active plan and a decided trade-off, a tree of another kind, and an archived conversation. Courses from earlier milestones sit archived in the switcher.

**The task course.** "Intro to Investing" is created empty before the session. Its material is a reading on long and short positions and short sales, taken word for word from the SEC's investor-education pages: a subject most participants can read without preparation, whose skills can be practised in a couple of minutes, so the sessions measure the app rather than the material. The participant brings it in during task 1.

## The task list

The tasks take about 40 minutes. Each later task uses what the earlier ones made. The instruction column is read to the participant as written; it states the goal, not the buttons.

| # | Task | Instruction to the participant | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bring in a material | "Here is this week's reading (a PDF). Add it to the Intro to Investing course so the app can work from it." | The app teaches from the learner's own files: is Materials the obvious place to start, and is it clear when the file is ready? |
| 2 | Grow skills from it | "Have the app suggest the skills this reading teaches. Keep the two you think matter most, in the topic you think fits." | The suggestion review and the tree carry the design: do they see that confirming a skill makes a leaf on a topic's tree? |
| 3 | Say how well you know one | "Tell the app how confident you already are about one of the two skills, and find out what it makes of that." | The starting confidence seeds the estimate: does the number, with its evidence and (i) notes, make sense to someone who has not read the design? |
| 4 | Learn with the tutor | "Learn the other skill with the tutor. When it makes a claim, check where in the reading that claim comes from." | Grounded tutoring is the app's unique part: are the citations noticed, followed and trusted? |
| 5 | Prove it | "Show the app you can do that skill without help, then find out how it judged your attempt and why." | Assessment closes the loop: is the help declaration understood, is the verdict's reason legible, and is the change in the estimate noticed? |
| 6 | Plan for a quiz | "You have a quiz tomorrow on these topics and about 15 minutes a day. Get a study schedule, and deal with whatever the app says will not fit." | Budgeting changed most since P1: are budget, exam and schedule found in that order, and is the trade-off proposal read as a choice? |
| 7 | Come back later | "Find out which of your skills you would forget first and when the app wants you to review it. Then make that topic's tree a cherry tree." | Memory explains the review schedule, and the tree picker is the one purely visual control; the owner's own test suggested it was hard to find. |

## Running a session

Each participant agreed to the session and to screen and audio recording before anything was shown; the recordings stay with the owner. The brief was that the app was being tested, not them, and that they should think aloud: what they were trying to do, what they expected, what they were reading. The facilitator stayed quiet, prompted only when they fell silent, and had a rule for help fixed in advance (after two minutes without progress, the smallest hint, written down with the task and time). Neither participant needed a hint. Notes were taken as one line per moment, and each session ended with a debrief on what worked, what did not, and what they had expected at each hesitation. The [session kit](P5-session-kit.md) holds the full script and the note sheet.

## Study reports

The two participants tested one after the other on the owner's laptop and account, with the SEC reading on short sales as the material. Neither received a hint. Both are students who had never used a study app, which is the intended audience at its least practised.

### Participant 1

P1 is a computer science student. She attempted all seven tasks. Tasks 1, 3 and 4 passed without comment, citations included.

The first stall was task 2. Asked to have the app suggest skills, she searched the course home and the tree before finding "Find skills" on the reading's own page, and called the button "not apparent" and the page cluttered. She looked where the goal lives, the tree; the action sits with the material because the design grows skills from a material, and nothing on the tree points back to it. A gulf of execution, crossed unaided.

Task 5 produced two moments. When she sent her practice answer, the app asked her to review it and tick a declaration before judging it, which she called "not fluid". After the verdict, "Keep learning" was hard to see, and pressing it opened a new conversation when she had expected to argue with the assessment in the same thread. Her model is one continuous conversation with a tutor; the design seals an assessed attempt so it cannot change under discussion, and nothing on screen says so.

On the Plan page she found the "Next up" banner wordy and the page cluttered, did not know what "Update plan" would do, and proposed dragging study sessions instead. Whether the trade-off proposal appeared was not recorded, so task 6's main question stays open.

What worked: she explored every feature unprompted and named the forest as what she liked most. In the debrief she said the Memory view had read to her as an overview of statistics rather than a place that shows what to review and how her memory is holding up.

### Participant 2

P2 is a mechanical engineering student. They tested after P1 on the same account and attempted all seven tasks. Their first comment came in task 1: they asked why "add material" and "import graded work" were separate buttons, chose the right one, and were not sure the split was wrong. Tasks 2 to 4 drew no comment.

The key moment was task 5. Their attempt was assessed as a gap. The verdict pointed them to the reading but did not say what the answer had missed, and they said the flow stopped there: they wanted the explanation, not a pointer. The rationale is written to justify the judgement and cite its source, and the follow-up lesson is a separate step the card does not make obvious. A gulf of evaluation: the app judged without showing reasoning they could use. P1 met the same card from the other side, wanting to answer back, which makes this the best-supported finding of the night.

In task 6 they read "this skill needs fresh independent evidence", said it made no sense and should go. The sentence names the design's idea of evidence rather than anything they could do. Whether the trade-off proposal appeared was not recorded. In task 7 they called the regrow and customise-tree controls hidden and awkwardly placed and proposed one control for a tree's look, which matches the owner's own earlier test.

What worked: the tasks before practice went through without comment, and like P1 they liked the forest. Like P1 they found parts of the interface cluttered; two separate sessions make that hard to dismiss as taste. In the debrief, asked about the layout, they said the chat layout was a bit confusing at first and that they liked the voice and attach-file buttons.

## Flaws and opportunities

Severity follows the directions: minor is friction the participant recovers from alone, moderate costs time but is recovered without help, major is recovered only with a hint, critical blocks progress without the facilitator.

| # | Flaw or opportunity | Why it happens | How a later design could address it | Level | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Find skills" cannot be found from the tree or the course home (P1). She looked where the goal lives, the tree, and the action lives on the material's page as one control among the viewer's others. | The design grows skills from a material, so the action was placed with the material. The participant's goal is phrased from the tree ("grow skills"), and the home page offers "Plant a topic" but no way in from a material. | A primary "Find skills" entry on the course home and on an empty topic, opening a material picker; and the button promoted on the material page. | Physical: the control exists and, once seen, was understood at once; it was not visible where she looked. | Moderate: she found it unaided, but only after searching, and the task gates every later one. |
| 2 | The assessment asks before it judges (P1): after sending an answer, a "review" step and a declaration checklist stand between the learner and the verdict. | The design records an assessment only with a declaration of whether help was used, so the declaration comes first. To the learner it interrupts what feels like a conversation. | Assess on send, with one "I used help" toggle beside Send, off by default; the checklist only when the toggle is on. | Conceptual: her model is a conversation, the design's is a sealed attempt with a qualified verdict. | Minor: she completed it, but it recurs on every attempt. |
| 3 | A weak verdict points to the reading but does not explain the gap (P2), and the way on, "Keep learning", is hard to see and starts a new conversation rather than letting the learner talk back (P1). | The rationale is written to justify the verdict and cite its source; the follow-up lesson is a separate session by design, because an assessed attempt must not change under discussion; the dispute path is a form. | The rationale says in two sentences what the answer missed, with the citation; the verdict card's main action is a reply box, "Ask about this verdict", that opens the follow-up lesson attached to the attempt, visibly in the same thread. | Conceptual: nothing tells the learner why the attempt is closed or what to do with the verdict. | Moderate: both could continue, but one lost the reason and the other the thread, at the moment learning should happen. |
| 4 | The app's internal vocabulary leaks: "this skill needs fresh independent evidence" (P2), "Update plan" (P1), "Fresh check". | The labels name the design's concepts (evidence, independence, recomputation) instead of the learner's action. The copy audit before the sessions found four names for one setting; this is the same habit. | Say the action: "Try this once without help to confirm the estimate". Remove "Update plan" and recompute whenever a saved input changes, which the app already does on save. | Linguistic. | Minor: both recovered by ignoring the words, but they recur across pages. |
| 5 | The Plan page says too much at once (P1, and both called the interface cluttered in places): the "Next up" banner, the exam, the budget, the fit and the sea compete, and the page reads as something to arrange rather than a readout. | The page composes four concepts (budget, exam, schedule, trade-off) and shows a status line for each; the copy audit cut repetition but not the number of things. | A one-line banner (the next activity and its minutes), the rest below the sea or in its labels; later, dragging an activity to another day as a pinned choice the next recompute keeps, which P1 proposed. | Physical. | Moderate: usable, but it cost her the sense of what the page was for. |

Also noted, below the cut: the tree's regrow and customise controls are in two places (P2; physical, minor; one "Tree" control would do); the separate "add material" and "import graded work" buttons could share one entry point with the choice as a second step (P2; conceptual, minor; the separation itself is deliberate); the Memory view read as a statistics overview rather than a review schedule (P1; conceptual, minor; its heading and first line should say what is due and when); and the chat layout confused P2 at first (physical, minor).
