## What changed since P4

The P4 submission froze the deployed app at `9255c1e` on the evening of October 26. The work that was finished that night but kept out of the submission, and the work since, is listed here with its commits.

- **Shorter words on the lesson page** (`965c64e`, merged in `49ffff3`). The four sentences under the Evidence heading and the basis sentence under "How the estimate got here" moved behind (i) buttons, and a sent message no longer carries a "Sending…" line, because the tutor's place already shows the wait. The server logs the reason when a tutor answer fails its checks, so a "could not produce a usable answer" on the deployed app can be traced.
- **The sidebar is the one place to pick or start a conversation** (`2f0a4b0`, merged in `21eb8f5`). The lesson page's tab strip listed the same conversations as the sidebar and grew with every lesson. It is gone; the Conversations group has its "+ New conversation" row back, a skill opens on its latest open conversation, and the composer's "+ Attach files" control, which was "+ Photo of work", takes any file.
- **Gemini 4 Argon at Medium is the default tutor model** (`82f2c82`, merged in `d0d0bc1`). In the final manual test before P4, answers from Gemini 3.8 Flash often failed the server's checks, while Argon's did not. `GEMINI_MODEL` still overrides the default, and a learner's own choice is kept.
- **A plan refusal names the review that blocks it** (`115610c`, merged in `8c2a982`). The planning concept refuses a new schedule while any of the learner's courses has a review in progress, but the Plan page only knew about the course on screen, so the learner read "Finish or stop the current activity" with nothing to find. The plan read now carries the blocking course and skill, "Update plan" says "Finish or stop the review of <skill> in <course> first." with a button that opens that course's plan, and a budget or exam saved during a review says the plan will update when the review ends.
- **A new lesson exists before the tutor answers** (`2635f37` and `89902f4`, merged in `cee72d4`). A new conversation used to be created on the server only after the tutor's first answer, so a learner who left the page before it, or whose tutor failed, lost the request. GroundedTutoring's `explain` became `requestLesson`, which saves the lesson from the learner's request at once, and `explainLesson`, which records the opening answer; `/learning/reply` asks for the opening the way it retries an unanswered question. The sidebar lists the lesson immediately, a failed opening leaves it waiting with a retry row, and a learner who leaves during the opening and comes back finds the page still waiting and then sees the answer arrive.

## Preparing the deployed app

The participants cannot have their own accounts, because sign-in goes through the class Commons and they are not in the class. Each session therefore runs on the owner's laptop, signed in to the owner's account, with the owner's courses as the realistic surroundings and one fresh course for the tasks.

**Realistic surroundings.** Before the first session, the account holds:

- three courses with real materials: lecture notes and problem sets as PDFs, one recorded lecture with its transcript, one set of returned graded work;
- ten to twenty confirmed skills per course, in three or four topics each, with estimates spread from new to mastered, several lessons and practice attempts with their conversations, and one or two adjustments with reasons;
- a daily budget and a dated exam in each course, with an active plan and at least one trade-off already decided;
- trees of different kinds per topic, and one archived course.

**The task course.** A course named for the participant's subject (the tasks below use Linear Algebra) is created before each session with nothing in it. The participant brings in the material in task 1. After the session the course is archived, so the next participant starts from the same empty state.

## The task list

The tasks take about 40 minutes. Each later task uses what the earlier ones made. The instruction column is read to the participant as written; it states the goal, not the buttons.

| # | Task | Instruction to the participant | Why it is in the list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bring in a material | "Here are the lecture notes for this week (a PDF). Add them to the Linear Algebra course so the app can work from them." | First contact with the app's main idea, that it teaches from the learner's own files. It shows whether Materials reads as the place to start, whether the import's progress is legible, and whether the participant can tell when the file is ready. |
| 2 | Grow skills from it | "Have the app suggest the skills these notes teach. Keep the two you think matter most, in the topic you think fits." | The suggestion review and the tree metaphor carry the design. It shows whether the participant understands that a skill becomes a leaf on a topic's tree, what confirming does, and whether the redesigned review form reads in order. |
| 3 | Say how well you know one | "Tell the app how confident you already are about one of the two skills, and find out what it makes of that." | The starting confidence seeds the memory estimate. It shows whether the estimate's number, its evidence and its (i) explanations make sense to someone who has not read the design. |
| 4 | Learn with the tutor | "Learn the other skill with the tutor. When it makes a claim, check where in your notes that claim comes from." | Grounded tutoring is the unique part of the app. It shows whether the composer, the sidebar's conversations and the source pane are discoverable, and whether the citations are trusted and followed. |
| 5 | Prove it | "Show the app you can do that skill without help, then find out how it judged your attempt and why." | Practice and assessment close the loop. It shows whether the help declaration is understood, whether the verdict and its reason are legible, and whether the estimate's change is noticed. |
| 6 | Plan for a quiz | "You have a quiz next Monday on these topics and about 20 minutes a day. Get a study schedule, and deal with whatever the app says will not fit." | Budgeting is the concept most changed since P1. It shows whether budget, exam and schedule are found in that order, whether the trade-off proposal is understood as a choice, and whether the sea view reads as a week. |
| 7 | Come back later | "Find out which of your skills you would forget first and when the app wants you to review it. Then make that topic's tree a cherry tree." | The Memory view and the Forest view explain the review schedule, and the tree picker is the one purely visual control. It shows whether the forgetting curve is read correctly, and whether the tree customisation is discoverable, which the owner's own test suggested it was not. |

## Running a session

- **Consent and recording.** The participant agrees to the session, to screen recording, and to audio, before anything is shown. The recording stays with the owner.
- **The brief.** The app is being tested, not the participant. They should say what they are thinking, what they are trying to do, what they expect to happen, and read aloud what they look at. Silence longer than about fifteen seconds gets a prompt: "What are you thinking now?"
- **When to help.** Only after the participant has been stuck for two minutes or asks for help a second time, and then with the smallest hint that lets them continue, never an explanation of the interface. Every intervention is noted with the task and the time.
- **Notes.** One line per moment: the task, the time, what the participant did or said, and what the app showed. Hesitations, backtracking, sighs and smiles count as moments.
- **Debrief, 20 minutes.** What worked, what did not, and for each noted moment of hesitation: what they expected, what the app did instead, and how they found their way.

## Study reports

To be written after the two sessions, 200 to 300 words each, balanced between what worked and what did not.

## Flaws and opportunities

To be written after the sessions: three to five, each with what it is, why it happens, how a later design could address it, its level (physical, linguistic or conceptual) and its severity (minor, moderate, major or critical).
