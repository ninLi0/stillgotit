## What changed since P4

The P4 submission froze the deployed app at `9255c1e` on the evening of October 26. The work that was finished that night but kept out of the submission, and the work since, is listed here with its commits.

- **Shorter words on the lesson page** (`965c64e`, merged in `49ffff3`). The four sentences under the Evidence heading and the basis sentence under "How the estimate got here" moved behind (i) buttons, and a sent message no longer carries a "Sending…" line, because the tutor's place already shows the wait. The server logs the reason when a tutor answer fails its checks, so a "could not produce a usable answer" on the deployed app can be traced.
- **The sidebar is the one place to pick or start a conversation** (`2f0a4b0`, merged in `21eb8f5`). The lesson page's tab strip listed the same conversations as the sidebar and grew with every lesson. It is gone; the Conversations group has its "+ New conversation" row back, a skill opens on its latest open conversation, and the composer's "+ Attach files" control, which was "+ Photo of work", takes any file.
- **Gemini 4 Argon at Medium is the default tutor model** (`82f2c82`, merged in `d0d0bc1`). In the final manual test before P4, answers from Gemini 3.8 Flash often failed the server's checks, while Argon's did not. `GEMINI_MODEL` still overrides the default, and a learner's own choice is kept.
- **A plan refusal names the review that blocks it** (`115610c`, merged in `8c2a982`). The planning concept refuses a new schedule while any of the learner's courses has a review in progress, but the Plan page only knew about the course on screen, so the learner read "Finish or stop the current activity" with nothing to find. The plan read now carries the blocking course and skill, "Update plan" says "Finish or stop the review of <skill> in <course> first." with a button that opens that course's plan, and a budget or exam saved during a review says the plan will update when the review ends.
- **A new lesson exists before the tutor answers** (`2635f37` and `89902f4`, merged in `cee72d4`). A new conversation used to be created on the server only after the tutor's first answer, so a learner who left the page before it, or whose tutor failed, lost the request. GroundedTutoring's `explain` became `requestLesson`, which saves the lesson from the learner's request at once, and `explainLesson`, which records the opening answer; `/learning/reply` asks for the opening the way it retries an unanswered question. The sidebar lists the lesson immediately, a failed opening leaves it waiting with a retry row, and a learner who leaves during the opening and comes back finds the page still waiting and then sees the answer arrive.
- **A starting confidence seeds at most a Good recall** (`c45b994`, merged in `00c8191`; policy `fsrs-2`). Under `fsrs-1` a reported confidence of 80 or more seeded an Easy first rating, whose stability is about 8.3 days, so a learner who said 90% saw a slow curve and a review more than a week out. Now 80 and above seeds Good (about 2.3 days), 40 to 79 seeds Hard (about 1.3 days), and below 40 seeds Again (about five hours), because saying you know something is weaker evidence than recalling it. Existing tracks are replayed under the new policy on their next read.
- **Reviewing suggested skills is a deck** (`b2ab024`, merged in `0d54147`). The review was a stacked form with no sense of place among the suggestions. It is now a card deck: a place marker ("Suggestion 2 of 3") with one pixel square per suggestion and previous and next arrows, the skill name as the card's title in the display face, a quoted line naming the material it came from, the topic as a row of pills with "New topic…", the outcome and the gate as labelled fields with hints and (i) bubbles, and the actions ranked: Discard quiet at the left, "Save as draft" secondary, "Confirm · add a leaf" primary. Edits survive moving between suggestions, and the lost-reply recovery banner is unchanged.
- **A selected tree stays open, and its sign offers "Change tree"** (`661cc59`, merged in `8c63cd9`). A tree used to open only while the pointer was over it, and the tree customisation lived only in the card under the grove, where the owner's own test did not find it. A tree picked out by a click, by "Show on tree" or from the Forest view's list now stays open until another is picked or the ground is clicked; hover still previews others. A small "Change tree" label with a pixel brush sits under the selected tree's sign on Home and opens the same picker. On touch screens a tap opens a tree and a tap on open ground closes it. In the Plan sea the whale rises toward the horizon and shrinks from two pixels per unit to one at the halfway point, snapped to whole pixels, and passes behind the buoys, the boat and the island. The unused `--today-column` token is gone.
- **The message box keeps an unsent message, and a reopened conversation shows its messages at once** (`a4f28e8` and `e2792e0`, merged in `93eb216`). An unsent message used to be lost when the page reloaded, the learner signed in again or switched courses. Each conversation now keeps its draft in the browser under the learner's name, so another learner on the same browser never sees it, and a new conversation keeps one draft per skill. A draft is let go only when the server accepts the message; if the page closes while a message is on its way, the next visit puts it back unless the server saved it. Drafts untouched for a week are dropped, at most fifty are kept, and attached files are not kept: the draft names them so the learner attaches them again. Separately, every message used to replay its rise-in when a conversation was shown again, which in a slow browser looked like two seconds of blank page. A reopened conversation now shows what it holds at once, and only new messages rise in.
- **Plain reads no longer wait for the tutor** (`bcfd07f`, merged in `f6522c7`; the archived list joined them in `3b058ca`). Every request about a learner's records waited for that learner's lock, and a tutor request holds the lock for as long as the tutor thinks, so the sidebar's conversations, Home's Continue card, the tree's estimates, the Memory view and the plan sat still until the answer arrived. These reads change nothing, so they now answer at once from the records saved so far, while every change still waits its turn. The skill page's own read keeps the lock, because it repairs evidence and closes submitted tasks that a failure left behind.
- **After a success, the Plan and the Memory view name the same review day** (`f444071`, merged in `3a6c777`). After a manual adjustment the Plan counted a skill's next review from its latest practice, while the Memory view counted from the adjustment, so the two could name different days; the Memory view also rounded up to whole 24-hour spans, so a memory due Friday evening, read on Wednesday morning, said Saturday. Both now take MasteryEstimating's due instant (the latest event, an adjustment included, plus the stability) and put it on a New York calendar day with one shared function. No formula changed, so the policy stays `fsrs-2`. The next-day review after a slip now counts calendar days, which fixes a date skipped or repeated across a daylight saving change. The Memory view says "Later today" for a memory due before midnight, and its flag reads "Review by" instead of the wrong "Below 70%".
- **Conversations can be archived, and older ones are reachable** (`39f1bb3`, merged in `0b25d7a`). The sidebar listed only a course's 40 most recent conversations and kept every one of them, so it filled with finished work and older conversations fell out of reach. Each row now has a "⋯" button with "Archive conversation", followed by a note with Undo. An archived conversation leaves the sidebar and Home's Continue card and waits under "Archived conversations", where it opens read-only and can be restored; an open lesson or practice task picks up where it left off once restored. Archiving is its own small concept, Archiving, bound as ConversationArchiving, so it never touches the dialogue, practice results, estimates or plan. A "Show older conversations" row reads the next 40.
- **A learner can delete a material** (`1b0edaa`, merged in `568e94e`). Materials has a "Delete material…" action, and its confirmation says what will happen. The material, its stored files and the text read from them leave the library, and the tutor stops using it for new lessons, practice and skill suggestions. Suggested skills from it that were never confirmed are discarded; confirmed skills keep their progress and list the source as "Deleted material". Past lessons stay readable: a citation of a deleted material says it was deleted instead of showing a broken passage. When every source of a skill is gone, the lesson page explains that the skill needs another source before its next lesson, and the server gives the same explanation. MaterialKeeping keeps only a short record that the learner's material in that course was deleted, so citations can say what happened and an old upload retry cannot bring the material back. A file the tutor already received stays in Google's store until it expires, within 48 hours.
- **A finished lesson can be continued** (`4295075`, merged in `69b3307`). Finishing a lesson used to close it for good, so a learner who thought of one more question had to start a new conversation, without the lesson's sources and earlier answers. A finished lesson now shows "Continue this lesson" as its main action, with "Keep learning" beside it. Continuing reopens the lesson where it was: GroundedTutoring's new `reopenLesson` sets its status back to open and changes nothing else, so the request, the opening explanation, its citations and the dialogue stay as they were, and the message box comes back with the cursor in it. The new `/learning/reopen` endpoint and its request admission turn down a lesson that is still open, a practice task, an archived lesson and another learner's session, with a sentence that says what to choose instead. A practice task never reopens, because its submitted attempt is the work its assessment judged, and an archived lesson is restored first and then continued. When every material behind a finished lesson has been deleted, the page explains that the tutor can no longer answer in it instead of offering Continue. Reopening changes no case, estimate or plan, because nothing in the progress and planning flows depends on whether a lesson is finished. The sidebar now reads its conversations again whenever the lesson page changes one or has a step refused, and its "… is archived. Undo" note goes away then, so Undo never acts on a conversation that is already back.
- **Importing the same graded paper twice no longer counts its marks twice** (`5db85ed`, merged in `0962c85`). Each upload used to be a separate source. Uploading a returned quiz again let every question be imported a second time, so the skill counted the same evidence twice, and the import screen could only warn about it. Now a paper is identified by its files. Every retained original already carries the SHA-256 digest of its bytes, and an imported question's source key now names the paper instead of the upload. AssessmentReviewing's existing rule of one case for each source therefore refuses the same question from another upload of the same files, whatever that upload is called. Request admission also declines reading or confirming a second upload of a paper whose marks were already imported. It does this before the tutor is contacted, with a sentence that names the earlier upload and the date of the import. The import screen shows that sentence where the questions would be, with a button to review the earlier upload and import any question that was skipped. Questions imported before this change are still recognized, and a retried upload or confirm still succeeds. A paper that is scanned again, or uploaded as a different set of files, has different bytes and is not recognized.
- **A learner's own attached work is no longer a course material** (`0b23bce`, merged in `6b9f978`). A photo or file attached to an answer used to be saved as an ordinary source, so it was listed among the course's files, counted in "N files", offered for Find skills and as a skill source, and could be taught from. MaterialKeeping now records each material's purpose, SOURCE or WORK, and the attach control saves its uploads as WORK. Lessons, practice, tutor answers, hints and skill suggestions select only sources. Proposing, editing or adding a skill source from work is refused with a sentence. The grader and lesson replies still read the work with the answer it belongs to. Materials lists the course's files alone and puts the learner's work in a collapsed "Your work" group below, newest first, where each file opens in the viewer and can be deleted but has no Find skills or "Ask about". Materials saved before this change read as sources. After a reload on Materials, the selected file now opens with its downloads and file kinds at once.

## Preparing the deployed app

The participants cannot have their own accounts, because sign-in goes through the class Commons and they are not in the class. Each session therefore runs on the owner's laptop, signed in to the owner's account, with the owner's courses as the realistic surroundings and one fresh course for the tasks.

**Realistic surroundings.** Before the first session, the account holds:

- three courses with real materials: lecture notes and problem sets as PDFs, one recorded lecture with its transcript, one set of returned graded work;
- ten to twenty confirmed skills per course, in three or four topics each, with estimates spread from new to mastered, several lessons and practice attempts with their conversations, and one or two adjustments with reasons;
- a daily budget and a dated exam in each course, with an active plan and at least one trade-off already decided;
- trees of different kinds per topic, and one archived course.

**The task course.** A course named for the participant's subject (the tasks below use Linear Algebra) is created before each session with nothing in it. The participant brings in the material in task 1. After the session the course is archived, so the next participant starts from the same empty state.

## The task list

The tasks take about 40 minutes. Each later task uses what the earlier ones made. The instruction column is read to the participant as written; it states the goal, not the buttons.

| # | Task | Instruction to the participant | Why it is in the list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bring in a material | "Here are the lecture notes for this week (a PDF). Add them to the Linear Algebra course so the app can work from them." | First contact with the app's main idea, that it teaches from the learner's own files. It shows whether Materials reads as the place to start, whether the import's progress is legible, and whether the participant can tell when the file is ready. |
| 2 | Grow skills from it | "Have the app suggest the skills these notes teach. Keep the two you think matter most, in the topic you think fits." | The suggestion review and the tree metaphor carry the design. It shows whether the participant understands that a skill becomes a leaf on a topic's tree, what confirming does, and whether the redesigned review form reads in order. |
| 3 | Say how well you know one | "Tell the app how confident you already are about one of the two skills, and find out what it makes of that." | The starting confidence seeds the memory estimate. It shows whether the estimate's number, its evidence and its (i) explanations make sense to someone who has not read the design. |
| 4 | Learn with the tutor | "Learn the other skill with the tutor. When it makes a claim, check where in your notes that claim comes from." | Grounded tutoring is the unique part of the app. It shows whether the composer, the sidebar's conversations and the source pane are discoverable, and whether the citations are trusted and followed. |
| 5 | Prove it | "Show the app you can do that skill without help, then find out how it judged your attempt and why." | Practice and assessment close the loop. | 6 | Plan for a quiz | "You have a quiz next Monday on these topics and about 20 minutes a day. ## Running a session

The [session kit](P5-session-kit.md) is the facilitator's working copy: the checklist for the deployed app and its data, the script read to each participant, the note sheet, and the templates the reports and the flaws are written from. The rules below are the ones it applies.

- **Consent and recording.** The participant agrees to the session, to screen recording, and to audio, before anything is shown. The recording stays with the owner.
- **The brief.** The app is being tested, not the participant. They should say what they are thinking, what they are trying to do, what they expect to happen, and read aloud what they look at. Silence longer than about fifteen seconds gets a prompt: "What are you thinking now?"
- **When to help.** Only after the participant has been stuck for two minutes or asks for help a second time, and then with the smallest hint that lets them continue, never an explanation of the interface. Every intervention is noted with the task and the time.
- **Notes.** One line per moment: the task, the time, what the participant did or said, and what the app showed. Hesitations, backtracking, sighs and smiles count as moments.
- **Debrief, 20 minutes.** What worked, what did not, and for each noted moment of hesitation: what they expected, what the app did instead, and how they found their way.

## Study reports

To be written after the two sessions, 200 to 300 words each, balanced between what worked and what did not.

## Flaws and opportunities

To be written after the sessions: three to five, each with what it is, why it happens, how a later design could address it, its level (physical, linguistic or conceptual) and its severity (minor, moderate, major or critical).
