## Updated journey

Maya is a second-year student taking linear algebra, and her midterm is in nine days. Matrix multiplication looked familiar in lecture, but the last problem set took her far longer than she expected. She worries that a weak foundation will cost her on the exam, and she does not know which part is weak.

She signs in with her 6.1040 Commons account and creates a course called Linear Algebra. On the course materials page, she adds the lecture PDF on matrix multiplication. The app reads the file and shows the text it could read, so she can check that nothing important was lost.

She asks the tutor to find skills in the lecture. It suggests a few, each with an outcome and a description of how she will show it. She rewords one, saves the drafts, and confirms "Multiply two matrices" and "Check matrix dimensions". Each confirmed skill appears as a new leaf on her home screen.

She says how confident she feels about each new skill. The app uses her answers as provisional starting estimates until she shows real work.

She opens the skill for multiplying matrices and asks for a worked example. The tutor explains the method from her lecture and lists the pages it used, so she can open the passage and read it herself.

Next she asks the tutor to check her understanding. It writes two new problems and a question about why the method works. She solves the problems on paper, attaches a photo of her work, and explains her reasoning aloud, and the app turns her speech into text that she can correct before sending.

She reviews the whole attempt and sends it for assessment. The tutor judges the work against the skill's requirements and explains its judgment.

Her estimate for the skill changes, and the skill page shows why. Its evidence timeline lists where the estimate started, how her attempt moved it, and how time without practice lowered it.

Later that week, her graded problem set comes back. She photographs the marked pages and imports them as graded work, together with the date she did the work. The tutor reads each question's mark and the grader's comment, and it proposes which skill each question shows and how well. She changes one mapping, keeps the others, and confirms. Each question now counts as evidence from the day she did the work, so a dimension mistake on one question lowers her estimate for checking dimensions.

She then adds the midterm to the planner, with its date and the two skills it covers. Her budget is 20 minutes a day, and the preparation does not all fit before the exam. The plan puts exam work first, lists the preparation it would leave out and what that costs, and changes nothing until she chooses to confirm those cuts.

Maya now knows that checking dimensions, not the multiplication itself, is her weak spot. Tomorrow's plan opens with a ten-minute review of that skill, and the rest of her exam preparation is laid out across the days before the midterm.

### Also in the beta

- **Managing skills.** After confirming a skill, a learner can rename it, move it to another unit, add another source, and revise its requirements. A revision becomes a new version: the earlier one stays readable as "Previous definition", and the new version's estimate starts from the old one and asks for a new check. A learner can also set prerequisites among confirmed skills. Practice on a skill then waits until its prerequisites are ready, and the message names the skill to work on first. Skills can be archived and restored.
- **Adjusting an estimate.** A learner who disagrees with an estimate can set it by hand and give a reason. The adjustment appears in the evidence timeline, and the plan is updated.
- **Emphasized units.** A learner can mark the units their professor emphasized. The course page shows the mark, but the plan does not use it yet.
- **Choosing the tutor.** The Tutor panel in the header lets a learner choose the Gemini model and how much it thinks before answering. The choice is saved on the server, so it follows the learner to every device, and each answer says which model and level produced it.
- **Any material the tutor can read.** Besides documents, slides, and notebooks, a learner can import scanned PDFs, HEIC and HEIF photos from a phone, and audio and video recordings. A recording is transcribed once at import into timed segments, so the tutor can cite a moment in it, and the original stays playable.
- **Several courses.** Each course keeps its own materials, skills, daily budget, and plan, and the course menu in the header switches between them.

## What changed since P2, and why

P2 ended with an alpha that ran only on a laptop and still lacked several features from the P1 pitch. The beta had two goals: to put the app online, and to finish those features. [P2](P2.md) listed what was still needed: "graded-coursework import in the UI, exam management and explicit tradeoff confirmation, richer prerequisite and requirements editing, a source-search fallback, and deployment." All of these are now in the beta except the source-search fallback.

### The first version of the beta

- **The app runs on the class platform.** The platform runs one Bun process on a read-only disk, with a single MongoDB server and S3 storage. The server now reads its address and origin from the platform, answers health checks, and saves uploads to S3.
- **Gemini replaced the ChatGPT connection.** The ChatGPT sign-in only worked on a local machine, so it could not run online. The app owner's Gemini key now powers every AI feature, including dictation. When the tutor fails, the learner sees why: the service is busy, the quota is used up, the answer was blocked, or the request timed out, among other reasons.
- **File import moved to TypeScript.** The platform has no Python or LibreOffice, so the importer now reads PDF text, images, text and code files, notebooks, and the text of Word, PowerPoint, and Excel files inside the server process. At first, ZIP archives and recordings were refused with a message that said so; recordings are now accepted, as described below.
- **Writes are safe without transactions.** The platform's MongoDB has no transactions. SkillMapping therefore writes records one at a time, in an order that a retry can finish, as [runtime responsibilities](design/runtime.md#safe-writes-without-transactions) explains.
- **GroundedTutoring is simpler.** Following TA feedback on P1, two unused actions, `askFollowup` and `clarifyInput`, were removed. Their checks depended on judging what a question means, which code cannot check. Lessons and practice sessions are now listed in the order they were started.
- **Several courses.** A learner can now create more than one course. Only one review can be in progress at a time across all of them.
- **Interaction fixes.** On a desktop, resting the pointer on a disabled button shows why it is disabled, except for the two buttons listed under Known limits. Each view shows success and error messages. Dates and source locations are readable ("Fri, Oct 23", "Lecture 4, page 1"), and keyboard focus returns to the control the learner used.
- **Visual design.** The first version's look came from the [visual study](design/visual-design.md): Georgia headings, Avenir Next text, clear buttons, and one cyanotype blue. Course materials became a library with the files, an "Add material" button with a drop area, and the skills grouped by topic. The chat screen followed the familiar layout of AI chat apps, and each leaf on the skill tree showed its skill's mastery as a shade of blue and a percentage. The interface has since been redesigned, as the last part of this section describes.

### Finishing the P1 features

- **Exams and trade-offs** (`02604d8`). The P1 pitch promised that exam work takes priority when time is short and that "You confirm those cuts before the plan changes." StudyPlanning already had these rules, but the interface offered only daily planning. The planner now lists a course's exams, and a learner adds one with its date and the skills it covers. Preparation for the nearest exam comes first. When it cannot all fit, a panel lists each cut with its minutes and consequence, and the learner either confirms the trade-offs or keeps the current plan. When the plan is urgent, exam preparation may skip the prerequisite check, as P1 allowed.
- **The evidence behind each estimate, and manual adjustment** (`cf90358`). The P1 pitch said to "Select it to see the mastery estimate and supporting evidence", and the stakeholder table said a learner "can correct an assessment or mastery estimate". The skill page now shows a timeline of every change to the estimate: its starting point, each assessed attempt, each adjustment with its reason, and time without practice. "Adjust estimate" sets the value by hand with a reason, apart from correcting an assessment. The same item lets a cited source open at the cited passage, lets one answer carry several photos, and adds Listen to practice questions and tutor replies.
- **The tutor model picker** (`5f2dc10`). In P2, learners chose a model and a reasoning effort through their ChatGPT plan. The move to Gemini removed that choice, because one server key now serves every learner. Bringing it back matters for two reasons. Quotas run out per model, so a learner can switch models when one is used up, and difficult material or grading may call for a more careful model, while routine practice can use a faster one. The Tutor panel offers three Gemini models and the thinking levels each supports, and it warns where a level may not finish within the 80-second limit.
- **Skill editing** (`7e05eae`). P2 listed "richer prerequisite and requirements editing" as missing, and the P1 sketch promised that a revised skill keeps its earlier score, labeled "Previous definition". The new Manage skills section covers renaming, moving, sources, revisions, prerequisites, emphasis, archiving, and restoring. Prerequisites now gate practice, and the refusal names the skill to work on first.
- **Graded-work import** (`249be27`). The P1 pitch said "Graded coursework can update relevant skills too", and the P1 journey has the step "Add coursework evidence". A learner uploads returned work with its date, the tutor proposes a skill and a result for each marked question, and the learner edits and confirms the rows. Each confirmed question becomes an assessment case dated to the day of the work, so the estimate's history reflects when the skill was shown.
- **Import anything the tutor can read** (`3198b4e`). The P1 pitch tells learners to "Bring notes, textbook passages, or another course document". The first version of the beta still refused scanned PDFs, HEIC photos, and recordings, because its own text extraction could not read them, even though Gemini can. The rule now is that a learner can import anything the tutor model reads. A PDF without a text layer becomes page locations that the tutor reads from the original pages, and HEIC and HEIF photos are kept as images. Audio and video are transcribed once at import into timed segments, outside any web request, so lessons, citations, and skill suggestions work from text as they do for documents. Files larger than the inline budget reach Gemini through its Files API. The upload limit rose from 25 MB to 95 MB, and every remaining refusal is a full sentence that says what to do instead. Graded work can now be a scanned PDF as well.
- **Refusal messages** (`0398d3f`). Before this change, most planning and learning refusals reached the learner as "Check the field and try again", because the engine keeps only a refusal's code. The host now adds the sentence that each design gives for the code, so the learner sees the actual reason. The same change put the seven new skill-editing endpoints under the learner's lock, which they had been missing.
- **sync-engine 1.2.0** (`d312f88`). The project moved from sync-engine 1.1.0 to 1.2.0. The checker reports the same two kinds of advisory as in P2. Their number rose from 40 to 66 only because of new work: each of the 18 new endpoints adds one endpoint-coverage warning, and each of the 8 new lists adds one list-order notice. The [diagnostic review](design/advisory-review.md) explains both kinds.

### Changes to the concepts, and why

- **Preferring is a new concept.** It remembers which offered option a user chose for one setting, on the server, so the choice follows them to every device. The tutor choice could have lived in the browser, but every tutor request runs on the server and must not trust a model name that the browser sends. Preferring stores options without interpreting them, and the application checks each choice against its model catalog. Because it knows nothing about models, it can serve any setting: its principle uses a made-up "Quick, Balanced, Thorough" setting.
- **SkillMapping keeps a version history.** Each requirements version now records the version it replaced, and a new query lists a skill's versions. This is what lets the course page show "Previous definition" and lets a revised skill's estimate start from the previous one.
- **SkillMapping units have an emphasis flag.** P1 promised that learners can mark the units their professor emphasized. The flag sits on the unit record, which SkillMapping already owns, because one flag with one action was the smallest change. The plan does not use it yet. Making planning use it means changing the candidate calculation, which the exam work was changing at the same time, so it is planned for P4.
- **MasteryEstimating's adjustment takes a key.** The database has no transactions, so a retried request must not add a second adjustment. The page keeps the key, value, and reason in session storage until the server answers, so even a retry after a reload sends the same key, and the concept records that adjustment only once.

## How the work was checked

> [Your words: one or two sentences on how the seven items were built (for example, with AI agents working from written designs) and what you checked yourself.]

Each of the six features was designed first in its concept specifications and composition documents. Each item, including the refusal-message change, was then built on its own Git branch and merged into `main` one at a time. After each merge, the generated files were rebuilt and `bun run check` was run. The full test suite passed after every merge from the second through the refusal-message merge, when it had 739 tests. Each of the six features also has a scenario test file that walks through its intended outcomes in order.

- **Exams and trade-offs.** HTTP tests cover setting and removing an exam, a proposal that needs confirmation, confirming and declining it, another learner's access, and the refusals for a past date, for no covered skills, and for confirming during an activity. The scenario has 17 tests. A review found that setting an exam in one course while an activity ran in another left the first course's plan out of date, so finishing or stopping any activity now refreshes every course with a saved budget. It also found that a refused replan turned the whole exam answer into an error even though the exam was saved. The exam reactions now skip the replan while an activity runs in another course, the answer says that the plan will update, and it does when that activity ends.
- **Evidence and adjustment.** Concept tests cover the adjustment, which is now part of MasteryEstimating's principle test. HTTP tests cover the timeline in the skill read, an adjustment's success and refusals, another learner's access, several photos in one answer, and the replan after an adjustment. The scenario has 9 tests. A review found two defects: a refused replan was not reported as the adjustment's answer, and an adjusted skill's review lost the window its evidence had set. Both were fixed with tests.
- **The tutor model picker.** Tests use a fake `fetch` to check catalog validation, that each request carries the chosen model and level, that an unsupported level is refused, that the default applies when nothing is chosen, that one learner's choice leaves another's unchanged, and the sentence for each provider refusal. The scenario has 10 tests. A review found that the page's label helpers had no test, so `tests/TutorChoice.test.ts` was added.
- **Skill editing.** HTTP tests cover each new endpoint's success and refusals, the new version's estimate, prerequisites in practice admission, archiving and restoring, emphasis, and another learner's access. The scenario has 22 tests. It found that a declined practice start used a general sentence instead of naming the prerequisite. The host's admission now names it, for HTTP and for the native gateway, and tests check both. A review also found that the revision test read the course before checking the estimates, so the read's repair could hide a missing estimate. A test now checks that a revision answers only after the new version's estimate is saved.
- **Graded-work import.** Tests use a fake tutor to check extraction answers, both valid and broken. They also check that confirming creates cases and updates mastery as of the work's date, that a repeated confirm is refused, that work with only a total score is refused, and that another learner is refused. The scenario has 18 tests. During the build, the engine could not start one action for each row of a list, so Confirm imports one question per request and the screen sends the checked rows one at a time. At the merge, extraction was routed through the learner's tutor choice, like every other tutor request.
- **Importing anything.** The tests listed under Tests cover each new kind of file, each limit's refusal sentence, the transcription worker, and the Files API, and the scenario has 12 tests. A review found that two identical recordings in one import left an import that could not be removed, so an import now keeps one recording per distinct file. It also found that retrying an attachment in a lesson could create a second "My work" material, so the page now keeps the request's identity until a material comes back.
- **Refusal messages.** Tests cover the sentence map, the codes whose sentence applies only on some endpoints, the cookie clearing for an ended session, the tutor's recorded reason, and the learner's lock on the skill-editing endpoints (`tests/Courses.http.test.ts:621`). `tests/RefusalDetails.http.test.ts` checks every sentence the host sends against the generated read-back of the designs.

## Tests

`bun run check` checks the concept sources against their specifications, confirms that the generated files are current, and type-checks the server and the interface. `bun run test` runs every test file against a real, standalone MongoDB server. Each file uses its own database, and no test calls Gemini.

The numbers here come from the run after the host-pass merge (`a40ccec`); the saved output will be regenerated after the last code commit. That run passed 739 tests across 67 files, with 7,389 assertions and no failures, in about 155 seconds. `bun run check` passed for 10 concepts and reported 66 advisories: 54 endpoint-coverage warnings, one for each endpoint, and 12 list-order notices.

## Known limits

**Importing**

- When an upload is refused while it is being read, because it has too many pages, is damaged, or has nothing readable, its files stay in storage, and there is no way to remove them.
- When one upload contains identical files, their text appears more than once in the material.
- The 80-second limit for one tutor call and the memory bound for the largest upload are tested with short stand-in values, not at their real sizes.
- GIF, BMP, and TIFF images are refused. Excel dates appear as serial numbers.
- A learner cannot delete a material once it is imported.
- Photos of the learner's own work are listed with the course materials.

**Graded work**

- One extraction reads at most 50 questions, so a longer paper must be split across uploads.
- Work that carries only a total score yields no rows, because a total cannot update individual skills.
- A PDF or photo larger than 15 MB cannot be read for marks.
- Uploading the same paper twice lets its questions be imported, and counted, twice.
- Whether a material is graded work is decided by its title, which starts with "Graded work: ". This kept GroundedTutoring unchanged, but a real material kind would be sturdier.

**Messages**

- Four refusal codes still reach the browser without a sentence: `MATERIAL_UNAVAILABLE`, `SESSION_CHANGED`, `SESSION_UNAVAILABLE`, and `SKILL_CHANGED`. `COURSE_UNAVAILABLE` has a sentence only on the planning endpoints, and `SKILL_UNAVAILABLE` only on the estimate adjustment. For these, the page shows its general message.
- Two disabled buttons, Start review in the planner and Find skills while drafts are saving, do not show their reasons to a mouse user.

**Skills and the course page**

- The skill tree shows at most 11 skills. Further confirmed skills appear only in the course materials and the Learn tab.
- A saved draft skill cannot be edited or discarded in the interface.
- Asking for skill suggestions again replaces suggestions the learner edited, without a warning.
- Marking a unit as emphasized has no effect on the plan.
- A course cannot be renamed.

> [Check after the redesign: whether the tree still shows at most 11 skills and whether the two disabled buttons above still hide their reasons; remove those lines if not]

**Planning**

- Each course has its own daily budget, and nothing limits the total across courses.
- After a learner declines a trade-off, the same cuts can come back after they finish or stop an activity in another course.

**Tutor choice**

- An answer loses its model label after a reload, because the label is not stored.
- Retrying a regrade after the provider refuses the request adds the learner's clarification a second time.
- Another browser's header shows the old choice until its Tutor panel opens or the page reloads.
- Only Pro at High is marked as possibly too slow, because the timings of each model and level have not been measured.

**Missing from the P1 pitch**

- A learner who has no material cannot choose a suggested reference.

## Plan for P4

- Delete the saved files of an upload that is refused while it is being read, so a refused upload leaves nothing behind in storage.
- Add the source-search fallback, so a learner with no material can choose a suggested reference.
- Let emphasized units raise the priority of their skills in the plan.
- Let learners edit and discard saved draft skills.
- Show every confirmed skill on the tree, not only the first 11.
- Give the remaining refusal codes their sentences.
- Fix the other limits above.
- Simplify the concepts: move the material library out of GroundedTutoring, drop proposal receipts, compute plan totals instead of storing them, give the review schedule its own concept, and write each reaction as an explicit when/where/then. The requirement versions stay, because the version history now depends on them.
- Prepare for the P5 user tests, so that a first-time tester can follow the journey on the deployed app without help.
